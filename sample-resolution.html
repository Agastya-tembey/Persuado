Humanity Without Borders

Committee: World Health Organisation (WHO)

Agenda: “Mental Health Action Plan: Promoting Care and Treatment”

Sponsors: Kingdom of Norway, Romania, Republic of Singapore

Signatories: Kingdom of Belgium, Kingdom of Bhutan, Federative Republic of Brazil, Kingdom of Cambodia, Canada, Republic of Chile, Republic of Cuba, Republic of Finland, Republic of Iceland, Islamic Republic of Iran, Republic of Iraq, Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan, Malaysia, United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland

Deeply concerned by the rising global burden of mental health disorders and the growing lack of access to proper treatment and support systems;

Recognizing that mental health is an essential component of overall public health and human well-being;

Alarmed by the shortage of mental health infrastructure, trained professionals, and affordable psychiatric care in many developing and underserved regions;

Acknowledging the severe psychological impact of armed conflict, displacement, cyberbullying, economic instability, and humanitarian crises on vulnerable populations;

Noting with concern the continued stigma surrounding mental illness, which prevents millions from seeking timely support and treatment;

Recognizing the increasing role of technology, telemedicine, and digital healthcare in improving access to mental health services worldwide;

Reaffirming the importance of international cooperation in strengthening mental healthcare systems, research, and crisis response mechanisms;

Recommends for the creation of an International Mental Health Solidarity Fund for accessing affordable medication in developing nations to be supervised by the WHO;

Encourages all member states to form media outreach programs, public education awareness under the initiative led by WHO titled “Voices of Resilience”;

Calls for the establishment of an International Mental Health Data and Research Alliance to monitor the emerging trends of global mental health;

Supports the development of an initiative, “Healing Beyond Words”, for a multilingual digital platform for anonymous counseling;

Requests the WHO to provide funding to expand the Mental Health Workforce for cross-border by the establishment of specialized Mental Health Emergency Response Teams;

Alarmed at the fact that Mental Health spending globally is still stuck at two percent of the health budgets of the nations around the world, especially the poor nations that spend only USD 0.02 on Mental Health care per citizen per year;

Recognizing that investing one dollar in mental health treatment will pay for five dollars in productivity from that treated individual.

Hereby Resolves,

  1. Proposes the integration of mental healthcare programs into primary healthcare systems through coordinated cooperation between member states, the World Health Organization (WHO), and relevant international health bodies to ensure that mental health treatment remains affordable, accessible, and non-discriminatory for all populations:

    a. Encourages the training of primary healthcare workers in psychological first aid, early diagnosis, and community-based treatment methods,

    b. Supports the development of affordable public mental healthcare infrastructure in underserved regions, particularly within low-income and developing nations;

  2. Urges the expansion of mental healthcare services currently concentrated in urban centers toward rural and marginalized communities through sustainable healthcare outreach programs:

    a. Encourages the establishment of mobile mental health clinics and outreach initiatives to improve accessibility in geographically isolated areas,

    b. Recommends partnerships between governments and local healthcare organizations to address shortages of trained mental health professionals;

  3. Calls for the establishment of mental health support systems within schools, universities, and educational institutions to assist students experiencing psychological distress and emotional challenges:

    a. Encourages the inclusion of counselling services, peer-support initiatives, and mental wellness education within academic institutions,

    b. Supports awareness campaigns aimed at reducing stigma surrounding student mental health and encouraging early intervention;

  4. Supports the development and implementation of telemedicine technologies and digital mental health platforms to provide counselling and psychological support for individuals residing in remote or underserved regions:

    a. Encourages international cooperation in improving digital healthcare infrastructure and internet accessibility,

    b. Recommends the implementation of privacy protections and ethical standards regarding patient confidentiality within digital healthcare systems;

    c. Requires all digital platforms under this initiative to utilize decentralized data storage networks to ensure full compliance with regional data sovereignty laws.

    d. Establishes a voluntary certification framework for AI-assisted mental health tools, requiring minimum standards of clinical validation, algorithmic transparency, human clinical oversight, and data privacy protection prior to deployment in member state health systems.

  5. Considers the establishment of international funding mechanisms led by economically developed member states, international financial institutions, and humanitarian organizations to improve mental healthcare systems within developing nations:

    a. Encourages transparent allocation of funding toward healthcare infrastructure, workforce training, and rehabilitation services,

    b. Mandates that recipient states ensure accountability and effective utilization of financial assistance toward long-term mental health development goals;

    c. Urges member states to establish legally binding requirements mandating mental health expenditure grow proportionally with national health budget allocations, ensuring financing survives fiscal austerity cycles rather than remaining subject to annual political discretion;

  6. Calls for the establishment of rehabilitation, trauma recovery, and psychological support programs for refugees, victims of armed conflict, and individuals affected by displacement and humanitarian crises:

    a. Encourages cooperation between humanitarian organizations and national governments to provide emergency psychological assistance,

    b. Supports specialized mental health initiatives focused on children, women, and vulnerable populations affected by conflict;

  7. Recommends the international sharing of medical research, scientific innovation, and technological advancements related to mental health treatment between member states and global medical institutions:

    a. Encourages collaborative research initiatives focused on improving diagnosis, treatment methods, and psychiatric care accessibility,

    b. Supports knowledge-sharing partnerships between developed and developing healthcare systems;

  8. Encourages the development of public awareness campaigns designed to combat stigma, discrimination, and misinformation associated with mental illnesses:

    a. Supports media campaigns, educational workshops, and community-led initiatives promoting mental health awareness,

    b. Encourages public figures, educators, and community leaders to advocate for open discussions surrounding psychological well-being;

  9. Urges governments, educational institutions, and technology companies to address cyberbullying, online harassment, and digital stress affecting young populations:

    a. Encourages the implementation of digital safety education and responsible social media usage programs,

    b. Supports stronger monitoring systems and reporting mechanisms to protect vulnerable individuals from online psychological harm;

  10. Supports the implementation of workplace mental health policies that promote employee well-being, equal treatment, and access to psychological support services:

    a. Encourages employers to establish counselling services, stress management initiatives, and reasonable workplace accommodations,

    b. Recommends protections against discrimination targeting employees experiencing mental health conditions;

  11. Recommends the establishment of emergency mental health response systems during natural disasters, armed conflicts, pandemics, and humanitarian emergencies:

    a. Encourages the inclusion of psychological support units within disaster response frameworks,

    b. Supports rapid deployment of trained mental health professionals during emergencies;

  12. Calls upon all member states to ensure that essential psychiatric medicines, counselling services, and humanitarian mental health assistance remain accessible during periods of conflict, instability, or national emergencies:

    a. Encourages international humanitarian coordination to prevent disruptions in mental healthcare delivery,

    b. Emphasizes the protection of vulnerable populations whose access to treatment may be threatened during crises,

    c. Strongly urges that humanitarian exemptions be explicitly applied to mental health medicines and resources in sanctioned nations, ensuring uninterrupted access to essential psychiatric care regardless of political circumstance.

  13. Calls upon all member states to refrain from imposing or maintaining unilateral coercive economic measures, including sanctions, that obstruct the ability of developing nations to procure psychiatric medicines, mental health technologies, and healthcare infrastructure, noting that such measures constitute a violation of the right to health under international humanitarian law.

  14. Emphasizes the need for sustainable and progressive financing in mental healthcare by urging all member states to allocate a minimum of 2.5% of national health budgets to mental health services as an immediate benchmark, with a progressive target of 5% in the long term in alignment with WHO recommendations, accompanied by mandatory annual reporting to WHO to ensure transparency, accountability, and measurable progress toward closing the global mental health treatment gap.

  15. Emphasizes that mental health policy implementation should not impose disproportionate financial obligations on low-income countries while maintaining adaptability to different socio-economic conditions and public health priorities of member states.
